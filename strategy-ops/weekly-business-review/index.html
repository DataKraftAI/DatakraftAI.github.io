Operating rhythm

A weekly business review that takes 30 minutes and changes decisions

Most weekly leadership meetings are status updates read out loud. A good one is shorter, starts with what is going wrong, and ends with decisions.

DataKraft · 6 min read

The weekly leadership meeting is the most expensive meeting in most companies: every senior person, every week, for an hour or two. Yet it often produces very little. Each leader reports on their area, the numbers on different slides do not quite match, and the real problems get five minutes at the end.

Here is the format I set up instead. It usually takes 30 to 45 minutes.

1. Agree on a handful of numbers

Pick five to eight KPIs that tell you whether the company is healthy this week. Each one gets a written definition, a single owner and a single source. If two people can produce different values for the same KPI, the definition is not finished.

KPI (example)OwnerSource
Monthly recurring revenueCFOBilling system, end of week
New customers this weekHead of SalesCRM, deals marked "won"
Customer churn, monthlyHead of CSBilling system, cancellations
First reply time in supportHead of SupportHelp desk, median in hours

2. Prepare in writing, before the meeting

By the day before, the numbers are updated in one shared view, with a simple status: green on target, amber within 10%, red more than 10% off. Each owner of an amber or red KPI adds two sentences: why, and what they propose.

Updates that need no discussion stay in writing. Nobody reads them out in the meeting.

3. Run the meeting in a fixed order

  1. Red first (15 minutes). Each red KPI: the owner's proposal, questions, decision.
  2. Decisions waiting (10 minutes). Anything that needs the whole group to decide.
  3. Amber and risks (5 minutes). Only if something changed since last week.
  4. Recap (2 minutes). Read back the decisions and owners.
If a week has no red KPIs, the meeting should be short. That is a sign the system works, not a missed opportunity.

4. Write down what was decided

Every decision goes into a log with an owner and a date, the same day. Next week's meeting starts by checking last week's actions. This alone removes most of the "didn't we already decide this?" moments.

What changes after a few weeks

  • Meetings get shorter, because updates move to writing.
  • Problems surface earlier, because red comes first and owners know it will.
  • The numbers stop being argued about, because they have one definition and one source.
