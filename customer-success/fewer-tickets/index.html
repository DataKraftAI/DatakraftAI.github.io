Support

Fewer tickets, not faster agents: fix the top five contact reasons

Most support teams are measured on how fast they answer. The bigger win is making sure customers do not need to ask in the first place.

DataKraft · 6 min read

When ticket volume grows, the usual answer is more people, more macros and tighter response-time targets. Those help, but they treat the symptom. In most support queues, a small number of reasons cause a large share of the tickets, and many of those reasons can be removed.

1. Tag a sample by the real reason

Take 200 to 300 recent tickets and give each one a reason, written from the customer's point of view: "reset email never arrived", not "login". Existing ticket categories are rarely good enough; they were usually chosen by whoever set up the help desk years ago.

This takes two or three focused hours. It is the most valuable time you will spend on support this quarter.

2. Rank the reasons and look at the top five

Contact reason (example)Share of ticketsFix belongs to
Password reset email never arrives14%Product / engineering
Where do I find my invoices?11%Docs + product (link in app)
Calendar sync stops working9%Engineering
How do I add a colleague?8%Help article + onboarding
Charged after cancelling6%Billing process

The numbers here are an example, but the shape is typical: the top five reasons often add up to around half of all tickets.

3. Fix each one where it starts

For every top reason, ask one question: what would have to change so that this ticket is never written? The answer is rarely "a better macro".

  • If the product confuses people, the fix belongs to the product team, with the ticket count as evidence.
  • If the answer exists but is hard to find, move it to where the customer looks: inside the app, next to the button.
  • If a process creates the problem (such as charging after cancellation), fix the process, not the reply.
A ticket is a customer telling you, for free, exactly where your product or process is unclear.

4. Make the rest easy to answer

Some questions will always come in. For those, a short help article and a matching reply template mean a new team member answers as well as the most experienced one. Link the articles from the contact form, so customers see the answer before they hit "send".

5. Track contact rate, not just response time

Add one number to the support dashboard: tickets per 100 active customers, per month. If it falls while the customer base grows, the work is paying off. Share the top five reasons with the product team every month, on one page.
