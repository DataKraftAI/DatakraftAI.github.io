Customer health scores that warn you before a customer leaves

Many customer success teams have a health score. Few trust it. The problem is rarely the data; it is a score built from too many signals that nobody ever checked against real churn.

DataKraft · 5 min read

A typical health score combines fifteen signals with weights someone chose in a workshop. Every account ends up somewhere around "amber", and customer success managers go back to their gut feeling. A useful score is simpler, and it is tested before anyone relies on it.

1. Start from the customers who already left

Take the last 20 to 50 customers who cancelled. Look at the three to six months before they left. What changed? In most products, a few patterns appear again and again.

Signal (example)Why it matters
Active users dropped by more than 30%The product is no longer part of their routine
The main contact left the companyNobody inside is championing the tool any more
Support tickets open for more than 10 daysFrustration builds before it is expressed
No login by an admin in 30 daysNobody is managing or expanding the setup

2. Keep it to three to five signals

Choose the signals that showed up most often before churn, and that you can measure automatically. Score each one simply: present or not. A customer with two or more warning signals is "at risk". That is a rule everyone understands, and anyone can check.

A health score is only useful if a customer success manager can explain, in one sentence, why an account is red.

3. Test it against the past

Apply the score to last year's customers. Would it have flagged most of the accounts that later cancelled? Would it have flagged many that stayed? If it misses most churned customers, change the signals. If it flags everyone, make the rules stricter.

4. Attach an action to every red account

  • Red for usage: offer a short training or a check-in on their goals.
  • Red for a lost champion: find and onboard the new contact within two weeks.
  • Red for open tickets: escalate internally and call the customer.

A warning without an action is just a worry. The score earns its place when every red account has a named owner and a next step.
