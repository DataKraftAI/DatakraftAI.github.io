Onboarding

A 30-day onboarding plan customers actually finish

Customers rarely churn because of one big problem. They churn because they never got properly started. The first month decides more than any renewal call.

DataKraft · 5 min read

Many onboarding plans are long checklists of product features. Customers tick a few boxes, get busy with their day job and quietly stop. A plan that works is shorter, built around the customer's goal, and has people, not just tasks, attached to it.

Start with one goal the customer cares about

In the kickoff call, agree one outcome for the first 30 days, in the customer's words: "our sales team sends all quotes from the new tool", not "complete onboarding". Everything in the plan should serve that goal. Anything that does not can wait for month two.

Name owners on both sides

Every task gets a name from the customer's team and a name from yours. Plans without customer-side owners are the ones that stall, because everyone assumes someone else is doing it.

The four-week rhythm

WeekFocusDone when
1Kickoff, goal, owners, access and data set upEveryone who needs access can log in
2Configure the one workflow behind the goalThe workflow runs end to end with real data
3Train the people who will use it dailyEach of them has completed the workflow once
4Go live, review the goal, plan month twoThe goal from week 1 is reached, or the gap is clear

A short call at the same time each week keeps the plan moving. Fifteen minutes is enough when the plan is clear.

Watch for early warning signs

  • A weekly call is cancelled twice in a row.
  • Fewer than half of the invited users have logged in after two weeks.
  • The customer-side owner changes, or stops replying.

Any one of these is a reason to call the customer's sponsor, not to send another reminder email.

Close the loop

At the end of the month, write a short summary: the goal, what was achieved, what is still open and what comes next. Send it to the customer's sponsor. It turns onboarding into a visible success, and it is the best possible start for the renewal conversation a year later.

Onboarding is finished when the customer reaches their goal, not when your checklist is complete.
