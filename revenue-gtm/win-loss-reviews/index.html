Win/loss reviews: what lost deals can teach you

Ask a CRM why deals are lost and the answer is usually "price". Ask the buyers and you often hear something quite different.

DataKraft · 5 min read

Most CRMs have a "loss reason" field. Sellers fill it in quickly, at the end of a disappointing process, and "price" or "no budget" is the easiest choice. Over time, the report says the company loses on price, and the conclusion is to discount. Often that is the wrong lesson.

Talk to the buyers, not just the sellers

Once a month, pick five to eight recent decisions, both wins and losses, and ask for a 20-minute call with the buyer. Someone who was not the seller should make the call; buyers are much more open with a neutral person.

  • What problem were you trying to solve when you started looking?
  • Which options did you consider, and how did you compare them?
  • What almost made you choose differently?
  • What was the deciding factor, in one sentence?
  • What could we have done better during the process?

Look for patterns, not anecdotes

One call is a story. Twenty calls are data. Tag each conversation with the real reason in the buyer's words, then count.

Stated in CRMWhat buyers actually said (examples)
Price"We could not see how it would pay off in our first year."
No budget"Our IT team was not convinced it would integrate with our systems."
Went with competitor"Their onboarding plan was clearer, so it felt less risky."

None of these is really about price. Each points to a different fix: a clearer business case, an integration story for IT, a stronger onboarding plan in the proposal.

"Price" is often the polite word buyers use for "we did not see enough value".

Share it with everyone who can act

A short monthly summary, one page, goes to sales, product, marketing and customer success. Each team takes away one thing to change. After three months, check whether the patterns moved.
