Forecasting

Why your sales forecast keeps missing, and three CRM rules that fix most of it

Forecasts rarely miss because the sales team is too optimistic. They miss because the data underneath them quietly goes stale. That part is fixable.

DataKraft · 6 min read

Most CRMs calculate a weighted forecast the same way: the deal amount multiplied by a probability for each stage. The maths is fine. The problem is that the inputs drift. A close date set three months ago is still there. A deal sits in "Proposal" because nobody moved it. A "next step" field was never filled in.

When I look at a pipeline that keeps missing, the same three issues explain most of the gap. Each has a simple rule that fixes it.

Rule 1: a close date in the past is a question, not a fact

In almost every CRM I have seen, a large share of open deals have a close date that has already passed. Each of those deals still counts towards this quarter's forecast, even though the date itself shows the plan did not work out.

The rule: a deal whose close date has passed is flagged automatically and counts at half its weighted value until the owner updates the date or closes it as lost. Every Monday, owners get one list of their overdue deals.

Counting at half rather than zero matters. It keeps the forecast realistic without punishing deals that are genuinely just a little late.

Rule 2: every stage has an exit rule a stranger could check

"Proposal" means different things to different people. For one seller it means "I mentioned pricing on a call". For another it means "a signed-off quote is with the buyer's finance team". Same stage, same probability, very different reality.

StageExit rule (example)
DiscoveryProblem, budget owner and timeline confirmed on a call
DemoDemo held with at least one decision maker present
ProposalA written price sent to a named decision maker
NegotiationBuyer has replied with changes to terms or price
Verbal yesDecision maker has said yes; only signature is open

Write these down, agree them with the sales leads, and put them in the CRM as the stage description. Then the probabilities finally mean the same thing for everyone.

Rule 3: no next step, no forecast

A deal without an agreed next step is usually a deal that has quietly stalled. In most pipelines, deals that leave a meeting without a booked follow-up are lost far more often than deals that have one.

The rule: when a deal changes stage, the CRM asks for the next step and its date. If there is no activity for 14 days, the deal is flagged and appears in the weekly review.

Then: one short weekly review

Rules only work if someone looks at the result. I prefer one 30-minute review per week, always from the same dashboard, in the same order:

  1. Flagged deals first: overdue, stale, no next step.
  2. Deals that moved stage since last week, and why.
  3. The forecast after the hygiene check, compared with the target.
Measure forecast accuracy every quarter. It is the only honest test of whether the process is working.

You can see how much these three rules change a forecast in the pipeline demo: edit a close date or add a next step and watch the numbers move.
