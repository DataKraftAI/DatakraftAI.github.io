Lead routing in an afternoon: faster replies without buying another tool

A new lead is never warmer than in its first hour. Yet in many teams it waits in a shared inbox until someone feels responsible. Here is how I set up routing with what a team already has.

DataKraft · 5 min read

Lead routing sounds like a software problem. Mostly it is a decision problem: who is responsible for which lead, by when, and what happens if they don't respond. Once those three answers are written down, almost any CRM or automation tool can carry them out.

1. Decide the rules on one page

Start with the simplest split that matches how the team actually sells. For most teams, two or three questions are enough:

QuestionExample answerGoes to
Is it an existing customer?YesTheir account manager
Company size?500+ employeesEnterprise team, round robin
Region or language?DACH, GermanDACH team, round robin
Anything else Inbound team, round robin

Resist the urge to add a rule for every special case. Each extra rule is another place where a lead can get stuck.

2. Set a response-time target everyone can see

Pick one number, for example "first personal reply within one working hour", and make it visible. A target nobody tracks is a wish. A target shown on the team dashboard every morning changes behaviour within weeks.

3. Always have a fallback

People are ill, on holiday or in back-to-back meetings. Routing breaks silently when it assumes everyone is available.

  • If the owner has not replied within the target time, the lead is reassigned to the next person in the rotation.
  • The team lead gets a short daily list of leads that were reassigned, so patterns become visible.
  • Absences in the shared calendar remove a person from the rotation automatically.

4. Build it with what you already have

Most CRMs have assignment rules and round robin built in. Where they don't, a small automation (Zapier, Make, or a short script) can read the form, apply the rules from step 1 and create the lead with an owner and a due time. That is an afternoon of work, not a new software purchase.

The goal is not clever routing. It is that no lead ever waits for someone to feel responsible.

5. Track one number

Measure the median time to first reply, per week and per team. If it drops, the routing works. If one team stays slow, you now know exactly where to look, and it is usually capacity, not motivation.

Routing is one part of keeping a pipeline healthy. The other part is what happens to deals later; see three CRM rules that fix most forecast misses.
