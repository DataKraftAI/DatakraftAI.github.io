Measurement

Did the AI tool actually save time? How to measure it honestly

"The team loves it" is not a business case. If an AI tool saves time, it can be measured, and measuring it honestly is what earns the budget for the next one.

DataKraft · 4 min read

Most AI pilots end with a demo and a positive survey. A few months later, nobody can say what changed. That makes the next project harder to approve, even if the first one worked. Four measurements avoid this.

1. Measure the baseline before you build

For one or two weeks before the pilot, record how long the task takes and how often it happens. A simple tally sheet is enough. Without a baseline, every later number is a guess.

2. Include the review time

If a person checks every AI result, that time belongs in the calculation. A tool that cuts writing time from ten minutes to one, but needs three minutes of checking, saves six minutes, not nine. Honest numbers are smaller, and far more convincing.

Reading and typing4.0 min0.3 min

3. Track usage, not just availability

A tool that is available to twenty people but used by four saves a fifth of what was promised. Track how many of the eligible tasks actually go through the tool each week. If usage drops, ask why before adding features.

4. Watch quality alongside speed

Time saved means little if errors go up. Pick one quality measure that matters for the task, such as corrections by reviewers, customer complaints or rework, and compare it with the baseline.

The goal is not to prove the tool works. It is to find out whether it does, while it is still cheap to change course.

Report it in one line

After a month: "The tool handles 85% of order emails, saves about 7 hours a week including review time, and corrections fell from 4% to 2%." That sentence is worth more than any demo.
