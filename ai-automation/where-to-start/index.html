Getting started

Where to start with AI in operations: boring, frequent and checkable

Many AI projects start with the most exciting idea and end as a demo nobody uses. The best first projects are the opposite: small, dull and easy to check.

DataKraft · 6 min read

Every team now has a list of things AI could do. The hard part is choosing the first one. A good first project builds trust in the technology, saves measurable time and teaches the team how to work with it. A bad one burns a quarter and leaves everyone more sceptical than before.

I use three questions to choose.

1. Does it happen often?

A task that happens 150 times a week saves more than one that happens once a month, even if the monthly one feels more painful. Frequency also means fast feedback: within a week you know whether the tool works.

2. Can a person check the result quickly?

This is the most important question. If a colleague can look at the AI's output and say "right" or "wrong" in a few seconds, you can safely start, because mistakes are caught. If checking takes as long as doing the work, the time saving disappears.

Easy to checkHard to check
Fields extracted from an order emailA strategy recommendation
A draft reply to a standard customer questionA legal assessment of a new contract
Tags and a summary for a support ticketA forecast for next year's revenue

3. How much is rules, how much is judgement?

If the task follows fixed rules and the input is structured, you probably do not need AI at all: a plain workflow automation is cheaper and more reliable. AI earns its place when the input is messy (emails, documents, free text) but the expected output is clear.

  • Fixed rules, structured data: workflow automation, no AI.
  • Clear output, messy text input: AI extracts or drafts, a person approves.
  • Real judgement needed: AI prepares, a person decides, or fix the process first.

Good and bad first projects

Good first projects sound almost boring: reading order emails into a system, tagging and routing support tickets, drafting replies to the ten most common questions, summarising calls into CRM notes. Bad first projects sound impressive: "an AI assistant for the whole company", "automated decisions on customer credit", "AI-generated strategy".

The goal of the first AI project is not to impress. It is to still be in use in three months.

Measure before you build

Before writing any code, measure how long the task takes today and how often it happens. Without a baseline, you cannot show the saving, and the next project will be harder to justify.
